# Calibration guide: evidence and limits

The guide supplies **general, non-AI possibilities**, not measured biomechanics, medical advice or a prescription. Most people use multiple joints. The in-app task measures cursor movement, not hand pose, grip, joint contributions, pain or fatigue. A relative mousepad label is not a standardized measurement. A model's personalized explanation can be wrong; compare reports with the actually supplied current-session scores. Stop if movement hurts and consult a qualified clinician for persistent symptoms.

## Sources

- Sanchis-Sanchis et al., [*Influence of Mouse Grip Type on Flicking and Tracking Tasks Performance*](https://doi.org/10.3390/app14167112), *Applied Sciences* 14(16), 7112 (2024). Twenty-two right-handed male regular FPS players used their preferred grip (3 palm, 9 claw, 10 fingertip). The study reported no significant differences in flick/tracking scores between grip groups, although some trajectory measures differed. Groups were small, unequal and self-selected, not a randomized test. It neither proves grips are equivalent for everyone nor identifies a universally fastest, most accurate or least fatiguing grip or sensitivity.
- Boudaoud, [*Mouse Sensitivity in First-person Targeting Tasks*](https://ieee-cog.org/2022/assets/papers/paper_64.pdf), IEEE Conference on Games (2022). The controlled first-person tasks illustrate task-dependent sensitivity and speed–accuracy trade-offs and a broad near-optimal range in that study. They do not establish a unique optimum for an individual or every game. A pad's usable travel, movement amplitude and game settings all matter; this study does not define universal pad-size categories.
- [OSHA computer workstation pointer/mouse guidance](https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations/components/pointer-mouse) discusses reducing reach and tight grip and maintaining comfortable wrist posture. It is workplace ergonomics guidance, not a gaming-joint comparison or an individualized fatigue prediction.

The card pictures are AI-generated illustrative examples, not photographs of the user, clinical anatomical diagrams, motion capture, or measurements. Descriptions such as “can support” or “may be tiring” describe possibilities and depend on the individual, task, practice, equipment and posture. There is no evidence here to rank wrist, fingers, arm or shoulder universally for flicking, tracking or fatigue.

The optional setup fields are exactly what the user types; there is no device or game detection. DPI and a game sensitivity are not sufficient to calculate physical cm/360 without the correct game-specific yaw coefficient and relevant settings. The guide does not make that calculation. Its game/DPI/sensitivity entries are independent from the separately controlled Settings In-Game Mode.
